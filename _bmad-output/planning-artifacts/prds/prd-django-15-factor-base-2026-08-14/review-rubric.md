# PRD Quality Review — django-15-factor-base (phase 1)

## Overall verdict

This is a strong, unusually honest PRD: the thesis ("the product is the set of decisions already made and proven", §1) is load-bearing rather than decorative, the trade-offs are named with what was given up (§11 dev/prod parity, no break-glass), and the substitution of verifiable criteria for adoption metrics (§7) is argued rather than assumed — the addendum §2 names the three metrics it rejected and why. What is at risk is concentrated in two places. First, the Success Criteria verify the *negative* authentication guarantee (refusals) and never the positive one — no criterion says a component successfully authenticates against the IdP, and 25 of the 49 FRs are referenced by no SC at all. Second, the refusal contract of §4.3 — the surface §11 says carries the whole guarantee — enumerates its credential-path condition exhaustively and omits the one credential path this product itself introduces: the local persona sign-in path of FR-19. Done-ness clarity is good on average and specifically weakest in §4.6, the group with the least existing code and the largest story volume.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions and located where a reader can argue with them. FR-12 does not merely require a check; it states that the previously implied mechanism (an assertion inside the deployed settings module) "inherits the hole it is meant to close" and relocates the evaluation. FR-13's Notes go further and revise an upstream artifact: "the refusal contract is **eight conditions**, not the six the brief's addendum §6.3 records," with the reason each was added and an instruction to reconcile the brief. §4.2 names four rejected alternatives (`mozilla-django-oidc`, `djangorestframework-simplejwt`, `django-oauth-toolkit`, `allauth.headless`) and points at where their reasoning lives rather than re-litigating it. That is the opposite of smoothing to neutral.

Trade-offs carry their cost. §11 "Dev/prod parity is deliberately traded (accepted)" states the factor it violates, its stock example, what the trade buys, and its bounds. "No break-glass account (accepted)" states that an IdP outage locks out the administrators of the IdP-dependent admin, and refuses to launder the local personas into a mitigation. FR-23's Notes explain why the requirement exists at all: "the failure is invisible to everyone who has network access."

The three Open Questions (§13) are genuinely open — each has an owner, a revisit trigger, and a decision it blocks, and none is answered in the following sentence. OQ-1 even pre-commits the shape of the answer ("the answer is a shorter session lifetime rather than a change to the mapper") without pretending to have made the call. The five live assumptions (§14) are all value-shaped, not decision-shaped, and each names what does *not* depend on it (NFR-1: "The requirement above does not depend on knowing it").

The one weakness is the single `[NOTE FOR PM]` in the document (§6.2, propagation to existing components). It is well-placed, but the PRD surfaces at least one other genuine product-level tension and hands it to architecture unflagged — see the materializer-cost finding below.

### Findings

- **medium** Materializer cost decision is foreclosed, not surfaced (§4.6 / §13 OQ-2 / addendum §1) — The addendum states plainly that if the strip/parameterize/keep disposition, fixture set, and feature-to-surface declarations are authored twice, "the materializer is throwaway scaffolding and its cost should be weighed accordingly." That is a product cost/benefit question, not purely an architecture one. The PRD marks §4.6 unconditionally **Phase-1 must-have** and files the question as OQ-2 owned by architecture, so the decision to spend the money is already made before the input that would inform it exists. *Fix:* add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` at §4.6 stating that the materializer's phase-1 justification holds regardless (the harness must precede the transition), but that its *scope* — how much is single-authored for phase 2 — is a cost decision the PM should see the answer to when OQ-2 resolves.
- **low** SC-3 is presented as machine-checked but nothing in this PRD checks it (§7) — The preamble asserts "The four criteria below are binary and machine-checked, and the harness of §4.6 is what checks them." SC-3 ("Containerized by CI and started on the target platform with no source edits") is not checkable by the harness; the harness materializes, gates, and smoke-checks, and never deploys. *Fix:* either scope SC-3 to what CI can prove (image builds, process model declaration present and well-formed, health endpoints answer in-container) or state explicitly that SC-3 is verified by first deployment rather than by the harness.

## Substance over theater — strong

No persona section exists, and the four JTBD groups (§2.1) each earn their place by driving requirements: the lead developer drives §4.1/§4.5/§4.6, the component developer drives all of §4.4, the platform group drives §4.6 and FR-35, and Operators — the group most likely to be furniture — drive FR-41 and FR-42 through a specific job ("Probe, drain, and restart any component the same way regardless of which capabilities it selected"). §2.2 Non-Users is doing real work, not padding: "Developers of components already generated" is what makes the §5 propagation non-goal legible.

The Vision (§1) could not be swapped into another PRD in this category. Its second paragraph makes a falsifiable claim about what the product *is* ("The product is not the Django code. Anyone could write that.") and its third paragraph states the ordering constraint that the whole of §4.6 exists to satisfy. The NFRs are the clearest evidence against boilerplate: NFR-6 converts the standard "overhead is acceptable" claim into a measurement obligation — "measured once against the reference application and the number recorded alongside the observability documentation, so the claim that it is acceptable rests on a measurement rather than on repetition." NFR-1 declines to state a startup budget and explains why the requirement survives without one.

The claimed novelty is modest and matches the evidence. §4.5 does not claim to have invented feature extraction; it reports a specific finding ("Removing MFA during the audit orphaned two template overrides that no import graph, linter, or dependency analyzer would flag. Only the coverage gate caught them, by reporting zero percent") and turns it into FR-28. That is Discovery output, not a differentiation section written because the template had one.

### Findings

- **low** Three Factor Coverage rows are asserted with no requirement behind them (§12) — Factors 1 (Codebase), 7 (Port binding), and 12 (Admin processes, which cites only FR-11 for a claim about management commands generally) carry a "Satisfied" status with no FR, no test, and no check. In a table whose stated purpose is to hold the product to the name it commits to, three unbacked rows weaken the table's status as a conformance claim rather than a narrative. *Fix:* either cite the mechanism that makes each true (e.g. the gunicorn/uvicorn process for factor 7 via FR-39) or mark them "asserted, unchecked."

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is stated and the arc follows it. §4.6's Description contains the sequencing argument in one paragraph — "phase 1 builds a materializer ... so the twelve-combination claim is provable *before* the transition rather than after it" — and §11 restates it as a risk with this PRD's ordering as the mitigation. The MVP scope kind is a coherent platform/enablement shape and the scope logic matches it: everything in §6.1 is either the reference application, the thing that proves the reference application, or the contract to a consumer outside the repository.

The counter-metrics are the strongest part. CG-1 through CG-4 each name a specific way a green build could mean less than it appears to, and each names what it counterbalances. CG-1 is not generic anti-gaming language — it identifies that template coverage *is* the orphan detector, so narrowing measurement "makes SC-1 pass and destroys SC-2." That is exactly the counter-metric role the rubric asks for, and it discharges the substitution of criteria for metrics honestly.

Coherence is held back by two things. The Success Criteria do not cover the arc they claim to close, and the priority scheme carries no sequencing information.

### Findings

- **high** The Success Criteria never verify that authentication *works* (§7) — Union the "Validates" lists: SC-1 (FR-29, 30, 31, 34), SC-2 (24, 27, 28), SC-3 (37–44), SC-4 (18, 19, 20, 32), SC-5 (12–17). FR-4 through FR-11 — the interactive flow, the programmatic flow, the shared mapper, per-authentication re-sync, the claims contract — appear in no criterion, and §4.2 states this is "the largest unbuilt block in the product and none of it is implemented." SC-5 verifies only that a deployed component refuses to authenticate outside the IdP; SC-4's "authenticates a persona" is the local substitution path, not the IdP. A build that satisfies all five criteria while the IdP flows reject every real token is possible on the face of this document. *Fix:* add a primary criterion covering the positive path — an identity presented through the interactive flow and the same identity through the programmatic flow both resolve to the same authorization state (FR-8's third consequence already describes the test) — and name FR-4 through FR-11 in it.
- **high** Twenty-five of forty-nine FRs are validated by no Success Criterion (§7) — Beyond §4.2: all of §4.1 (FR-1, 2, 3 — the immovable core), all of §4.8 (FR-45, 46, 47 — observability, which §4.8 calls "the one capability a developer cannot accidentally work without"), all of §4.9 (FR-48, 49), and FR-21, 22, 23, 25, 26, 33, 35, 36. §7 frames these criteria as the definition of success; as written, half the requirement set sits outside that definition. *Fix:* either add criteria for the immovable core and observability, or state in §7 that the criteria are a chosen sample of the highest-consequence claims and that per-FR consequences carry the rest — right now the reader is invited to infer the former.
- **medium** Priority carries no sequencing (§4, all groups) — Eight of nine feature groups are **Phase-1 must-have**, with a single partial **Next** (FR-43's scheduling). "Everything is must-have" is a scope statement, not a prioritization, and the groups have real dependencies among them: §4.6's harness cannot materialize combinations without §4.5's declared surfaces, and §4.3's refusals cannot be written before §4.2's credential surface is rewired. §0 hands sequencing to the architect, but nothing in the PRD tells the architect which order the product prefers if the whole set does not fit. *Fix:* add a one-paragraph sequencing note stating the dependency order among the groups and which group the PM would defer first if phase 1 has to be cut.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

The average FR here is well above what story creation usually receives. FR-2's consequences name six OpenTelemetry packages by distribution name and state the exact combinations each must appear in and be absent from. FR-6 anticipates the most likely wrong test — "A test asserts the route's absence from the resolved URL configuration, not merely the setting's absence — removing the authentication class disables token *acceptance* and leaves the route still issuing them." FR-28 specifies a mutation test ("An orphaned template override introduced deliberately into a combination causes that combination's gate to fail") rather than asserting that coverage is measured. FR-9 distinguishes the addition and the *removal* of group memberships and requires a test that mapping runs on a second authentication, not just the first. FR-12's fail-closed rule is unambiguous and comes with the exact state its test must construct.

The failures are not distributed evenly. They cluster in §4.6 — the group with the most net-new code and no existing implementation to anchor against — and in the places where the PRD names a contract without naming what carries it. Two of the PRD's central artifacts, the feature model and the per-combination process declaration, are referenced as though they exist and are never given a location, format, or owner, while FR-8 by contrast pins the mapper to `src/config/authorization/` and defends the choice. The asymmetry is the tell.

Non-functional bounds are handled honestly: §6.2 explicitly de-scopes probe timings, startup budget, termination grace, resource limits, and JWKS cache TTL to architecture, and §8 states the behavioural contract without them. That is a deferral, not an adjective.

### Findings

- **critical** The refusal enumeration omits the one credential path this product creates (FR-13, second bullet; Glossary "Persona"; CG-4) — FR-13 enumerates its credential-path condition exhaustively — "This covers `ModelBackend` present in `AUTHENTICATION_BACKENDS`; a non-empty `ACCOUNT_LOGIN_METHODS`; `DJANGO_ADMIN_FORCE_ALLAUTH` not true; `rest_framework.authtoken` installed or `TokenAuthentication` in the DRF defaults; and the `obtain_auth_token` route reachable" — and the local persona sign-in path of FR-19 is not among them. The Glossary asserts a persona "Exists only where the refusals do not apply" and CG-4 asserts "The four substitutions are bounded and each is guarded by a refusal," but no condition guards this one. Trace the four: sqlite → FR-13 bullet 1; in-process cache → FR-14; eager execution → FR-14; local personas → nothing. FR-20's local keypair is guarded (the JWKS trust-anchor condition), which makes the persona omission look like an oversight rather than a deliberate reliance on FR-17's allowlist. §4.3's own words describe the consequence: "a credential path the check fails to inspect ... puts local authentication into a deployed environment — and that vulnerability would be one this product created deliberately rather than inherited." FR-36's strip list also does not remove the persona-seeding development task from materialized output, so the command ships. *Fix:* add a ninth unconditional condition — the persona authentication path installed, or the persona seeding command reachable, in a deployed component → refusal — and update §4.3's "eight conditions", FR-16, SC-5, §6.1 and §12 factor 9 to match. If the intent is instead that the persona path is caught by FR-17's allowlist, say so explicitly at FR-19 and CG-4, because an enumerated "This covers ..." list reads as the complete definition to whoever writes the test.
- **high** The feature model has no carrier (FR-1, FR-24, FR-27, FR-29, FR-30) — FR-1's second consequence is "No feature toggle in the feature model can be set to a value that removes any of the above"; FR-24 requires that "Each feature declares both its package surface and its non-package surface"; FR-30 requires materialization to fail when "A parameter added to the order surface" has no fixture. None of these is implementable without an artifact: no file, format, schema, or location is named anywhere in the PRD, and no FR requires one to exist. §13 OQ-2 asks whether the declarations are single-authored for phase 2 — which presumes an artifact the PRD never creates. *Fix:* add an FR to §4.5 requiring a declarative feature-model artifact at a named location, stating what it declares (feature → package surface, non-package surface, constraints) and that both the materializer and the refusal/allowlist tests read it rather than duplicating it. Every FR above then becomes testable against a file.
- **high** FR-13's JWKS trust-anchor condition states no comparison (FR-13, fifth bullet) — "The JWKS trust anchor is not the configured IdP → refusal." Nothing states how a component determines that: URL host equality with the issuer, scheme (a `file://` or `localhost` JWKS location), a discovery-document round-trip, or an allowlist of issuers. FR-16 requires "Each of the eight conditions has at least one test that configures the forbidden state" and this is the one condition whose forbidden state cannot be written down from the PRD. §4.3 singles it out as the subtlest of the eight ("the component is simply anchored to a key that was generated onto a developer's laptop"). *Fix:* state the rule — e.g. the JWKS URI must share scheme and host with the configured issuer and must not resolve to a local scheme or loopback host — or record it as an explicit `[ASSUMPTION]` owned by architecture, so it is not silently left to the implementer of a security control.
- **high** "Stable enough" is the entire specification of the provenance stamp (FR-35) — "The stamp's location and format are stable enough that an external process could enumerate components by version." This is precisely the adjective-in-place-of-bound pattern, and it lands on the artifact the addendum §2 calls "the join key that would make estate-wide analysis possible at all" and that §5 relies on to make the propagation non-goal defensible. Two implementers will produce two incompatible stamps and both will satisfy the sentence. *Fix:* name the file, the format, and the required fields (accelerator version, selection set, generation timestamp), and require that the format be machine-readable.
- **high** FR-17's allowlist scope is ambiguous in a way that breaks every generated component (FR-17) — The FR headline scopes it to "the component's authentication surface," but the first consequence reads "`AUTHENTICATION_BACKENDS`, the DRF default authentication classes, and the resolved URL routes each match an approved allowlist; an entry present but not listed fails the test." Read literally, *every* resolved URL route must appear in an allowlist — so the first business route a developer adds to their generated component fails the build. That inverts the product's central promise ("The first commit its author writes is business logic", §1). *Fix:* scope the route clause to credential-issuing and authentication routes (or to routes under the auth namespaces), and state that application routes are out of the allowlist's scope.
- **medium** FR-27's second consequence describes an undecidable check (FR-27) — "No template or static asset unreachable from any view in the combination is present." Static reachability analysis of Django templates is exactly the analysis §4.5 says does not work: "no import graph, linter, or dependency analyzer would flag" the orphaned overrides; "Only the coverage gate caught them." FR-27 asks a story author to build the tool the PRD just said cannot be built, when FR-28 already carries the working mechanism. *Fix:* restate FR-27's second consequence as a declared-surface check (nothing from an unselected feature's declared non-package surface is present) and let FR-28's coverage signal carry residue detection.
- **medium** "Equivalent" is undefined in the one place it is the acceptance test (FR-29) — "Materialized output for the all-features-selected combination is equivalent to the reference application." It cannot mean identical: FR-36 strips `_bmad/`, `.claude/`, `.agents/` and parameterizes `pyproject.toml`, `README.md`, `sonar-project.properties` and the package path `src/django_service/`. So the comparison is "identical modulo the strip and parameterize lists" — which is a testable statement and should be the one written. *Fix:* state the equivalence relation explicitly in terms of FR-36's three lists.
- **medium** Documentation appears as a consequence five times with no home and no check (FR-11, FR-22, FR-36, FR-40, FR-43) — "Documentation states the bootstrap path", "Documentation states the constraint's scope explicitly", "Pruning is documented as a scheduled admin process", "Documentation states that the deployment pipeline runs migration before new pods begin serving", "Documentation splits". In four of these the documentation statement is one of only two consequences, so it carries half the FR's done-ness. No file is named (though FR-36's Kept list implies `docs/development.md` and `docs/observability.md`) and nothing in the gate checks it. *Fix:* name the target document for each, and add a gate step or checklist item so "documented" has a verifiable state.
- **medium** FR-49 has no testable consequence (FR-49) — Its two consequences are a historical report ("Object storage was the first test of this rule and it held") and a process statement ("A proposed feature whose dependencies are absent from the channel forces an explicit supply-chain exception decision rather than a silent addition"). Neither is a condition a build can be in or out of. This is a policy — correctly stated in §9 Constraints — wearing an FR number that will become a story nobody can close. Addendum §4 supplies the missing mechanism (read the specific build's `depends` list from `api.anaconda.org/package/conda-forge/<name>`; compare builds, since fixes arrive as build-number bumps at an unchanged version), which never made it into the FR. *Fix:* either fold FR-49 into §9 and renumber, or give it a consequence — a documented check procedure and a recorded result per feature, checked in with the feature-model declaration.
- **low** FR-32's second consequence is a design constraint, not a smoke-check outcome (FR-32) — "Neither the database backend nor the authentication mode is treated as a feature toggle; they are properties of the environment a combination runs in, so the combination space stays at twelve." True and worth stating, but it is not something the smoke check can pass or fail. *Fix:* move it to §4.6's Description or to the Glossary's *Combination* entry.
- **low** FR-3's "template rendering works" (FR-3, first consequence) — Sitting among four precise conditions ("the admin renders, static files serve, the messages framework is available"), the last one is unbounded. *Fix:* name the assertion — e.g. an admin page renders with a non-empty body and no `TemplateDoesNotExist`.

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are stated, not inferred, and they are stated at the point where a reader would otherwise assume. §5 carries ten explicit non-goals, each with the reason it is out rather than a bare list: the propagation non-goal names the shape it would take if it were in scope ("the shape `cruft` and `copier update` take for their own template systems — a second product with its own lifecycle"), and the local-IdP-container non-goal concedes it is "the highest-fidelity local option" before rejecting it and says where it remains right ("as an optional path rather than a requirement"). §2.2 Non-Users independently fences the same boundaries from the user side.

Per-FR `Out of Scope` blocks appear exactly where an omission could be silently assumed. FR-9 excludes propagating revocation into an established session and tags the assumption that makes that acceptable. FR-13 excludes detecting the development keypair *file* in a built image and explains why the remaining condition is the broader one. FR-23 excludes environment installation ("which downloads packages by definition. The claim begins once the environment exists") and a general no-network guarantee, naming what a full guarantee would cost ("a network-denied smoke check; that was considered and set aside as disproportionate"). De-scoping is proposed with its consequence, not performed silently.

Open-items density is low and appropriate for a launch-grade, chain-top PRD: three Open Questions, five live `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, one `[NOTE FOR PM]` — nine items across 49 FRs, none of which blocks architecture from starting, and §13 says so explicitly ("None blocks architecture from starting; each blocks a specific decision inside it"). All five assumptions are value-shaped (a TTL, a session lifetime, a set of writable paths, a grace period, a startup budget) rather than decision-shaped, and §14's preamble records five assumptions that were *promoted to requirements* during review — which is the discipline working in the right direction.

### Findings

- **low** FR-43's priority is stated two ways that do not agree (§4.7 header, FR-43 Priority note) — The group header reads "**Priority: Phase-1 must-have**, except FR-43 which is **Next**"; FR-43's own note splits it ("setting the session engine explicitly is **phase-1 must-have**; scheduling the pruning process is **Next**"). §6.1 and §6.2 follow the split version, and §12 factor 6 depends on the engine being in scope. The header is the one that is wrong, and it is the one an epic-planning pass reads first. *Fix:* change the header to "except the scheduling of session pruning in FR-43, which is Next."

## Downstream usability — adequate

For a chain-top PRD this is largely well-built. The Glossary (§3) is 22 terms deep, and §3's preamble makes verbatim use a stated discipline rather than a hope ("introducing a synonym anywhere in this PRD is a discipline violation"). It goes further than most, encoding disambiguation rules directly into entries: "**IdP** ... Never used to abbreviate anything else"; "**Enterprise developer portal** ... Always written out; never abbreviated". The hard distinctions the whole document rests on — *reference application* vs *component* vs *template*, *combination* vs *valid combination*, *refusal* vs *default* ("a refusal cannot be reached accidentally"), *selection* vs *verification* — are all defined and held.

FR IDs are contiguous and unique across FR-1 to FR-49, with no gaps or duplicates. Every FR carries an explicit `Realizes UJ-n`, and all three UJs are realized. Each UJ has a named protagonist (Dana, Marco, Priya) whose context travels inline, and each has climax, resolution, and — for two of three — an edge case. Sections stand alone: cross-references go through Glossary terms and FR IDs rather than "see above."

What holds this to adequate rather than strong: two stale cross-references, an undefined domain noun in three surface forms, a vocabulary slip that contradicts the Glossary's own discipline, and a duplicated requirement that will produce two stories for one piece of work.

### Findings

- **medium** FR-25 and FR-33 are the same requirement in two groups (§4.5, §4.6) — FR-25 "The broker constraint is enforced at selection" has two consequences: the space is twelve, and "The materializer refuses the invalid pairing with a stated reason." FR-33 "The materializer refuses invalid combinations" has two: the pairing is refused before any source is produced, and the refusal names the broker constraint. These are one behaviour with one implementation, split across two feature groups, and story creation reads FR headings as story seeds. *Fix:* keep FR-33 as the implementation requirement and reduce FR-25 to the selection-space statement, cross-referencing FR-33 — which is what FR-25 is already trying to do through a broken reference (below).
- **medium** "Approved channel" is load-bearing and absent from the Glossary (FR-2, FR-48, FR-49, §9, §10, §2.2) — It appears as "the approved channel" (FR-48), "the approved package channel" (§2.2, §10), and "the channel" (FR-48's exception narrative, §9). It is the subject of an entire feature group and of factor 2, and it is the one term where the PRD needed the naming discipline §3 imposes everywhere else. *Fix:* add a Glossary entry naming the channel and fixing one surface form.
- **low** "Feature toggle" contradicts the Glossary's own definition of *Feature* (FR-1, FR-32, FR-43) — §3 defines a Feature as "Selected or absent; never present-and-disabled," and §4.5 restates it ("Unselected features are **absent**, not present-and-disabled"). Three FRs then reason in toggle language: "No feature toggle in the feature model can be set to a value that removes any of the above" (FR-1); "Neither the database backend nor the authentication mode is treated as a feature toggle" (FR-32); "session hygiene a property of an unrelated toggle" (FR-43). FR-32's usage is arguably deliberate (it is denying toggle-hood), but FR-1's is not. *Fix:* rewrite FR-1's consequence as "No selection in the feature model removes any of the above," and leave FR-32's denial with a parenthetical noting the term is used only to reject it.
- **low** Two stale FR cross-references (FR-18, FR-25) — `prd.md:338` reads "The smoke check of FR-33 passes for all twelve valid combinations"; the smoke check is **FR-32** (FR-33 is the materializer's invalid-combination refusal). `prd.md:418` reads "The materializer refuses the invalid pairing with a stated reason (FR-34)"; that is **FR-33** (FR-34 is verification-coverage reporting). Both are off by one in the same direction, which suggests an FR was removed from §4.6 after these were written — worth a sweep of that group. SC-4 correctly cites FR-32, confirming FR-18 is the stale one. *Fix:* FR-18 → FR-32; FR-25 → FR-33.

## Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product. This is a capability spec for an accelerator, and it is built as one: features grouped by capability with FRs nested, a Factor Coverage table doing conformance-traceability work, and NFRs stated as system-wide invariants. The UJ downscaling is not just defensible, it is *defended in place* — §2.3 opens by stating why three journeys exist and what they are for ("the ordering surface is the developer portal (out of scope) and the primary product surface is a repository, so these three journeys exist to anchor the *why* behind the requirements rather than to feed UX work"). A reader who expected UJ density is given the reason they will not find it, at the moment they would look. Neither over- nor under-formalized.

Brownfield handling is better than most. §0 declares tense discipline explicitly — "present tense describes what is true in the repository today. *Must* and *will* describe what this PRD requires and what is not yet built" — and the document holds it. Existing code is cited at line granularity (`production.py:26-28` as the built refusal, `sonar-project.properties:6` as the hardcoded project key, `users/admin.py`'s `secure_admin_login` as an existing mechanism FR-7 reuses "and requires no new code"). Feature groups state their build status up front: §4.2 "This is the largest unbuilt block in the product and none of it is implemented"; §4.3 "One condition is built; seven are not, and the mechanism that evaluates all eight is not built either"; §4.8 "Largely satisfied today; this group states what must not regress and what must be added." §12's Status column carries the built/unbuilt distinction into the conformance table. New and existing behaviour are never conflated.

§2.3's promise that undeliverable journey beats are marked is kept where it matters — UJ-1 carries the portal-generation caveat inline — and UJ-2 and UJ-3 genuinely need no mark, since the materializer delivers both.

### Findings

- **medium** Two "Satisfied" statuses are contradicted by the PRD's own supporting material (§12 factors 2 and 4) — Factor 2 reads "Declared and lock-pinned, resolved from the approved channel with no exceptions; no system packages (FR-48) | Satisfied", but addendum §4 states the opposite about the repository as it stands: "`pixi.toml` still declares the dependency under `[pypi-dependencies]` (line 90) ... Moving the dependency and updating both comment blocks is repository work, deliberately not performed during this PRD run." FR-48 also requires "A test or gate step asserts that the package-index dependency block is empty," which does not exist. Under §12's own definition — "*Satisfied* means the mechanism exists in the repository today" — factor 2 is not satisfied; the *decision* is made and the change is pending. Factor 4 has the same problem in a milder form: it claims object storage attaches by environment variable while FR-49 reports only that the storage packages are *available on the channel*. *Fix:* mark factor 2 "Resolved upstream, repository change pending (addendum §4)" and factor 4 "Satisfied for database and cache; object storage not yet built."

## Mechanical notes

- **Status vocabulary in §12 is unbounded.** The legend defines one value — "*Satisfied* means the mechanism exists in the repository today" — and the column then uses six: `Satisfied`, `Satisfied, declared`, `Decided, not implemented`, `Decided, engine not yet set explicitly`, `Traded, knowingly`, `Designed, not implemented`. Architecture will read this table for sequencing, and the difference between "Decided" and "Designed" is currently unknowable. Define the vocabulary or collapse it to three values.
- **Combination arithmetic is inverted in FR-43.** "background task processing exists in only eight of the twelve combinations, and a component whose session table grew without bound in the other four would make session hygiene a property of an unrelated toggle." Under the broker constraint, background task processing requires the Redis cache, so it exists in **four** valid combinations (2 UI × 2 object storage) and is absent from **eight**. §4.3 uses the same arithmetic correctly ("An unconditional refusal there would reject four valid combinations" — the four with no Redis). The error strengthens FR-43's own argument rather than weakening it, but combination counts are load-bearing throughout this PRD and a story author deriving a matrix from FR-43 will get it wrong. Swap the two numbers.
